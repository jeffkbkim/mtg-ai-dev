## Arena Powered Cube primer

The format: a 540-card singleton cube of Magic's most powerful cards (the Power Nine, Moxen, Sol Ring, Mana Crypt,
fetch lands and dual lands), drafted and played best of one with 40-card decks. Every card in both decks is strong;
there is no filler. Games are short (a median of six turns) and are often decided by tempo and fast mana, not by
long combat races. Your deck's plan (at the end) says how your deck wins; follow it.

Mandatory checks before each decision:
- What does each player's deck want to do next turn, and can I stop it or race it? Count the damage both ways.
- What could the opponent have? Free spells need no untapped mana: Force of Will and Force of Negation (exile a blue
  card), Daze (return an Island), Snuff Out, Solitude (exile a white card), Subtlety, Fury, Grief, Fireblast
  (sacrifice two Mountains). Open blue mana also means Counterspell, Mana Drain, Mana Leak, Memory Lapse, Remand,
  Spell Pierce, Miscalculation, Brazen Borrower. Sweepers: Wrath of God, Toxic Deluge, Damn, Sunfall, Balance,
  Upheaval. Do not commit more threats than you need into a likely sweeper; bait counters with the lesser spell.
- Spend mana every turn if you can, and in the right order: mana sources first (land, Mox, Sol Ring) so the turn's
  spells are castable, then the spells that matter most. Leave mana up only for a specific instant you hold.

How the strong cards work:
- Moxen and Sol Ring/Mana Crypt/Mana Vault: play them at once; they are free tempo. Black Lotus, Lotus Petal and
  Lion's Eye Diamond are one-shot: spend them on the turn they win the game or make a play the opponent can't answer
  in time (turn-one three-drop, Show and Tell, a reanimation, a big storm turn), not on a small spell.
- Fetch lands (Polluted Delta and friends, Prismatic Vista, Fabled Passage): crack them for the land your hand needs
  next (dual lands count as both types). They cost 1 life and thin the deck.
- Wasteland and Strip Mine destroy a nonbasic land: hit a dual or a land the opponent's colours depend on early.
- Tutors (Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, Enlightened Tutor, Mystical Tutor, Green Sun's Zenith, Natural Order): fetch
  the card that wins or the answer to the threat that beats you, not the most powerful card in the abstract.
- Planeswalkers: use an ability every turn. Attack the opponent's planeswalkers before they take over; protect yours
  by keeping blockers back.
- Graveyard decks: Entomb, Reanimate, Animate Dead, Necromancy, Unburial Rites with Griselbrand, Archon of Cruelty,
  Atraxa, Grief; Show and Tell and Sneak Attack put Emrakul or Griselbrand in for free. If the opponent fills a
  graveyard with a huge creature, answer the reanimation or remove the card from the graveyard (Scavenging Ooze,
  Dauthi Voidwalker, Soul-Guide Lantern).
- Storm and Breach: Dark Ritual, Lotus Petal, Lion's Eye Diamond, Underworld Breach, Brain Freeze, Tendrils of Agony
  win in one turn once enough spells chain. Count your storm before you start; keep the kill spell for last.
- Artifacts: Tinker puts a big artifact in play; Urza's Saga fetches a zero- or one-cost artifact; Mox Opal needs
  three artifacts. Combos: Dark Depths + Thespian's Stage or Vampire Hexmage make a 20/20 Marit Lage.
- Aggro (Ragavan, Monastery Swiftspear, Adeline, Goblin Rabblemaster, burn: Lightning Bolt, Chain Lightning,
  Fireblast): curve out and keep the pressure, burn the face when it wins the race or removes the only blocker.
- Control (Jace the Mind Sculptor, Teferi, counters, sweepers): trade one for one early, win with card advantage.

Mulligans (best of one): keep a seven with two or more lands or a land plus fast mana and something to cast in the
first two turns. A hand that only works if the opponent does nothing is a mulligan; a slow hand is fine against an
unknown deck if it has interaction.
