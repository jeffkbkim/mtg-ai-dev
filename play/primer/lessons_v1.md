## Lessons from earlier games: check these before acting
1. Library clock: before any optional draw, mill, surveil or manifest, compare both libraries. If the opponent can't
   kill you soon, your library is the clock: skip optional card flow. For mill, draw or discard effects, choose the
   target player on purpose.
2. "Tapped out" only rules out tricks. Before attacking, list every untapped potential blocker, including double blocks
   and face-down creatures that could turn face up, and ask whether you can afford the worst block.
3. Count the opponent's untapped lands and cards in hand. If an instant or flash play would turn the attack into a
   blowout, attack only with creatures you can afford to lose.
4. Check the crack-back: which of your creatures will be tapped on their turn, and does their counter-attack cost you
   more than your attack gains?
5. Chip damage needs a reason. When you are ahead, or the game will be decided another way, don't send a valuable
   creature into a bigger blocker or a first striker for a few points.
6. Protect what's worth more than a card: a creature that runs your engine, carries your auras or holds an exiled card
   should only risk dying for a bigger payoff.
7. Trust the power, toughness and keywords shown in the state: they already include every effect. Don't recompute them
   from card text.
8. Read the whole interaction: before removal or a trick, check what the opponent's permanents give back (a token, a
   card, a trigger), and prefer the target that shuts off their engine.
9. Unused mana is wasted: before passing with mana open, name what you hold it for; otherwise use it, and don't keep
   taking damage while holding removal.
